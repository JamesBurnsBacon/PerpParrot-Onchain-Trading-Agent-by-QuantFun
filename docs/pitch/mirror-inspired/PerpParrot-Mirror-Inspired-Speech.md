## 1 · The hook

PerpParrot helps you understand what several traders’ positions mean together. Instead of checking accounts separately and reconciling conflicting positions, you see one combined plan, who contributes, and how it changes. Today, you can explore our read-only paper demo.

## 2 · The customer’s question

You are watching three traders. Their contributions point in different directions: six hundred dollars long, two hundred short, four hundred long. Looking at each account separately does not tell you the combined position. 

## 3 · The reveal

They add up to eight hundred dollars long. The short offsets part of the longs. One planned Bitcoin position replaces three conflicting contributions. These numbers illustrate the calculation; they are not actual trades. Now, the real demo.

## 4 · Who shapes the plan

A final number hides where it came from. Here the contributors stay visible. This capture has six selected sources. The largest share is twenty-three point two percent. A share weights a trader’s positions before they are combined. You can see whose positions shape the plan, rather than only seeing its final total.

## 5 · What the plan contains

Two questions matter: which direction, and how much? This view answers both in one place. Bitcoin is planned long at two point one percent of portfolio value; Litecoin is short at one percent. Blue and red show direction. The percentages show exposure, not expected returns. You can understand both direction and size.

## 6 · Why these traders

Why those sources? The review asks three questions: does the trader fit, what could go wrong, and what might have been missed? Three AI contexts use one provider. They support source review; they do not guarantee protection or profitable copying.

## 7 · Understand a change

Return to our example. Trader A exits. The plan drops from eight hundred to two hundred dollars long. A’s share is not reassigned to the others. With B and C unchanged and no cap adjustment, you can explain why the plan changed.

## 8 · Inspect activity

Before judging a result, check whether the system ran, and where it stopped. Each cell represents one scheduled run. Green shows completed dry-run activity; red shows failures. This demo makes interruptions visible. A green cell is not proof of a funded fill or a successful investment.

## 9 · Inspect the outcome

Finally, read the paper result. This earlier capture shows the model changing over time, with modeled fills and costs. It is useful for inspecting simulated behavior. It does not establish live returns or predict performance.
